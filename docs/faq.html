Questions, care & maintenance

The things customers ask most, answered straight.

Scheduling & process

How long does the whole project take?
Typically two to three weeks from template to installation. Fabrication itself is seven to ten business days. Exotic materials we have to source can add a week. If you have a hard deadline, put it on the booking form and we'll tell you honestly whether we can hit it.
What is a "template" and why can't you just use my measurements?
A template is a precise digital map of your cabinets as they were actually built — including the walls that aren't square, which is nearly all of them. We laser-measure on site and cut to that map. Working from a homeowner's tape measurements is how gaps at the wall happen. Your numbers are great for an estimate; they can't be used to cut stone.
Do my cabinets really have to be installed first?
Yes, fully installed and level. Stone is cut to the cabinets, so if the cabinets move afterward the stone no longer fits — and stone can't be adjusted on site the way laminate can. This is the single most common cause of delays.
How long will my kitchen be without a sink?
Usually about 24 hours. The old tops come out and the new ones go in the same day, but the adhesive and silicone under the sink need overnight to cure before plumbing is reconnected. Plan for one night of dishes in the bathroom.
Can I change my mind after template?
Up until we cut, yes — but changes to material, edge, or layout after template mean re-templating and can affect price and schedule. Once the slab is cut, changes mean a new slab. Get your decisions settled at or before template.
Do you work with contractors and designers?
Regularly. If you're coordinating with a general contractor, put their name in the notes on the booking form and we'll schedule directly with them so you're not relaying messages between trades.

Pricing & payment

What does the estimate on the booking form actually mean?
It's a ballpark built from your square footage, material, and the extras you selected. It is not a quote. Slab selection alone can swing the number significantly — two granites at the same shop can differ by $30 a square foot. Your firm price comes after template, when we know the exact layout and slab.
Why is countertop pricing by the square foot of slab, not counter?
Because we buy and cut whole slabs. A sink cutout doesn't return usable material, and an odd layout can leave large unusable remnants. Pricing on slab area consumed is how the cost of the actual stone gets reflected honestly.
What deposit do you take, and when?
50% at template, once the final price is confirmed and you've approved the layout. The balance is due on installation day. Nothing is due to submit a booking request.
What adds cost beyond the per-square-foot price?
Sink and cooktop cutouts, premium edge profiles, tear-out and disposal of your old tops, plumbing disconnect and reconnect, stone backsplash, waterfall panels, and any structural support needed for large overhangs. All of these are on the booking form so your estimate accounts for them.
Is there a minimum job size?
Yes. Small jobs still require a template trip, a slab, shop time, and an install crew, so very small projects carry a minimum. A single bathroom vanity is absolutely worth doing — just expect the per-square-foot math to look different than a kitchen.

Seams, quality & warranty

Will my countertop have a seam?
If any run is longer than about ten feet, yes — slabs are roughly 120 by 65 inches. We place seams where they draw the least attention, usually at a sink or an inside corner, and we show you the planned locations at template. A well-made seam is a thin line you can feel more easily than see, but no seam is invisible. Anyone promising otherwise is overselling.
What if something isn't right?
Tell us before we leave, or call the same week. Fabrication and installation defects are ours to fix, and we fix them. What we can't warranty is damage from use — a chip from a dropped pan, a crack from someone standing on an overhang, or etching on marble that we told you would etch.
Do you warranty the work?
We stand behind our fabrication and installation. Engineered quartz carries its own manufacturer warranty, which we'll register for you. Natural stone is a natural product — fissures, pits, and color variation are characteristics, not defects, and we'll point out anything notable on your slab before we cut it.
Can you repair countertops you didn't install?
Usually. Chip repair, seam refresh, re-polishing, and resealing are all things we do on existing stone. Send photos by email and we'll tell you what's realistic before anyone spends money on a service call.
Care

Living with your countertops

Stone is durable, not indestructible. Five minutes of habit protects a serious investment.

Every day

Warm water, a soft cloth, and a pH-neutral cleaner made for stone. That's genuinely it. Wipe spills as they happen — especially wine, citrus, coffee, and oil.

Never use

Vinegar, lemon, bleach, ammonia, glass cleaner, bathroom scrub, or any "all-purpose" spray with an acid in it. They strip sealer and etch polished stone permanently. Skip abrasive pads too.

Sealing

Granite and quartzite: about once a year. Marble: twice a year. Quartz and porcelain: never. To test, leave a few drops of water on the surface for fifteen minutes — if it darkens the stone, it's time to reseal.

Heat

Granite, quartzite, marble, porcelain, and soapstone handle hot pans. Engineered quartz does not — the resin scorches and the damage is permanent. Use trivets on quartz, always.

Cutting

Use a board. Not because you'll hurt the stone — you'll destroy your knives on it, and on softer stone like marble and soapstone you can leave marks.

Weight and edges

Don't sit, stand, or kneel on countertops, especially near a sink cutout or an unsupported overhang. That's where stone breaks. Don't let cabinet doors swing into an exposed corner.

Getting a stain out of natural stone

Most stains lift with a poultice: mix baking soda and water into a thick paste, spread it about a quarter-inch over the stain, cover with plastic wrap taped at the edges, and leave it 24 to 48 hours. As it dries it pulls the stain up out of the stone. Oil-based stains may take two rounds. Re-seal that spot afterward. If it doesn't budge, call us before trying anything harsher.
