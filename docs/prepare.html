Checklists

Getting ready

Most delays come from a site that wasn't quite ready. Ten minutes with these lists keeps your project on schedule.

Before we measure

Template day checklist

Template is where your final price and your exact stone layout get locked in. It takes about an hour for a typical kitchen.

Cabinets installed and level

This is non-negotiable. We measure the cabinets as they actually sit, so they must be fully installed, shimmed, and level before we arrive. If they move after template, the stone won't fit.

Sink and faucet on site

Have the actual sink and faucet in the house, in the box. We template the cutout to your specific sink — a different model with the same nominal size won't necessarily fit.

Cooktop on site

Same reason. Bring the unit or at least its printed cutout template. Downdraft units especially need to be measured, not assumed.

Counters cleared

Empty everything off the existing tops and out of the sink base cabinet. We need clear access to every surface and the wall behind it.

Decisions made

Edge profile, seam preferences, backsplash height, and overhang depth all get confirmed at template. Bring your questions — changes after this point cost money.

A decision-maker present

Someone who can approve the layout and sign off needs to be there for at least part of the appointment. Deposits are collected at template.

Install day

Installation checklist

Plan on four to eight hours for a typical kitchen. It's dusty and loud — this is a good day to be elsewhere with the pets.

✓

Clear a path from the driveway to the kitchen

Slabs are carried by hand and can weigh several hundred pounds. Move furniture and anything breakable out of the route. We lay down floor protection.

✓

Empty the base cabinets under the counters

Especially the sink cabinet. We work inside it to set clips, plumbing, and supports.

✓

Disconnect the plumbing beforehand — or ask us to

Water and disposal must be disconnected before old tops come out. We can handle it if you checked that box on the booking form.

✓

Expect dust

We vacuum and cut wet where we can, but tear-out and on-site edge work make dust. Close doors to adjacent rooms and take down anything hanging on shared walls.

✓

Leave the plumbing off for 24 hours

Silicone and adhesive need to cure. Reconnecting the sink too early is the single most common cause of a seam problem. We'll tell you exactly when it's safe.

✓

Walk the job with us before we leave

Check the seams, the edges, the overhangs, and the cutouts while we're still on site. Anything that isn't right is easiest to fix before the truck pulls away.

Estimating

Measure your own countertops

You don't need to be precise — you need to be close. We do the real measuring at template. This is just to get you a number for the booking form.

1

Break the kitchen into rectangles

Every countertop is a set of straight runs. An L-shaped kitchen is two rectangles. A U-shape is three. An island is its own rectangle. Sketch them on paper.

2

Measure each run in inches

Length along the wall, and depth from the wall to the front edge. Standard counter depth is 25½ inches including the overhang. Islands are usually 36 to 42 inches deep.

3

Multiply and convert

For each rectangle: length × depth ÷ 144 = square feet. A 10-foot run at standard depth is 120 × 25.5 ÷ 144, which is about 21 square feet.

4

Add them up, and don't subtract the sink

Total all your rectangles. Leave the sink and cooktop areas in the number — stone is priced on the slab area consumed, and cutouts don't give material back.

5

Where overlapping corners meet, count them once

On an L-shape, the corner square belongs to one run or the other, not both. Counting it twice inflates your estimate by a couple of square feet.

A quick sanity check

Most kitchens land between 30 and 60 square feet. A small galley kitchen is around 25 to 30. A large kitchen with a generous island runs 60 to 90. If your number is far outside that, re-check whether you divided by 144.
