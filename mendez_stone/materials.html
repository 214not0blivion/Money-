Materials guide

Choosing your surface

There is no single best countertop — there is the one that matches how you cook, how much upkeep you will actually do, and what you want to spend. Every sample below is drawn to show how the stone really behaves.

Natural stone

Granite

Heat-proof, hard to scratch, and no two slabs alike. Slightly porous, so it wants sealing about once a year. The workhorse, and usually the best value in natural stone.

Engineered

Quartz

Ground quartz bound in resin. Non-porous, never needs sealing, and the colour is consistent slab to slab. Not heat-proof — the resin scorches, so use trivets.

Natural stone

Marble

Nothing else looks like it, and it stays cool for pastry. It also etches from lemon and wine. Choose it knowing it earns a patina — that is the deal.

Natural stone

Quartzite

Harder than granite, often looks like marble, and resists etching far better. Dense and difficult to cut, which is why it costs more.

Sintered slab

Porcelain

UV-stable, heat-proof, stain-proof and thin. Excellent outdoors and for waterfall panels. Edges show a colour core unless mitered.

Natural stone

Soapstone

Non-porous and acid-proof — lemon juice does nothing to it. Darkens with mineral oil, and scratches sand right out.

Edge profiles

The edge changes the whole feel of a countertop. Standard profiles are included in the per-square-foot price; premium profiles add about $12 per linear foot. Shown in cross-section at 3 cm.

Standard

Eased (Square)

A crisp square edge with the sharpness taken off. Modern, clean, the most common choice.

Standard

Quarter Round

Top corner rounded, bottom square. Softer than eased, and crumbs wipe straight over it.

Standard

Half Bullnose

The top half rolled into a curve. Forgiving on hips, and it sheds water well.

Standard

Beveled

A 45° chamfer along the top. Traditional and crisp, though it shows chips more.

Premium

Full Bullnose

The whole edge rolled into a half circle. Very safe, and it makes the top read thinner.

Premium

Ogee

An S-curve. Formal and ornate — best on thicker stone and classic kitchens.

Premium

Mitered / Built-Up

Two pieces joined at 45°, so a 3 cm top reads as a solid 2–4 inch block.

Premium

Waterfall

The stone turns down the side of an island to the floor. Priced per panel.

Thickness, finish & seams

The three questions that come up at every measure.

Thickness

3 cm is our standard — it sits straight on the cabinets and gives a substantial edge. 2 cm costs less and suits bathrooms, but needs a plywood substrate and usually a built-up edge to look right in a kitchen.

Finish

Polished is glossy and easiest to clean. Honed is matte and hides fingerprints. Leathered is textured with a slight sheen and hides just about everything — our favourite on dark granite.

Seams

Slabs run about 120" × 65", so a run longer than ten feet gets a seam. We put it where it draws least attention — usually at a sink or an inside corner — and show you the spot before we cut. A good seam is felt more than seen, but no seam is invisible.

Overhangs

Up to 10" of unsupported overhang is fine in 3 cm stone. Past that you need corbels or steel, which we spec at the measure. Standard counters overhang the cabinet face by about 1½".
